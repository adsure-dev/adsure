Reading your heatmap

Four questions to ask about spots 1, 2 and 3.

Is spot 1 your product or offer?

If the first look goes to a background, a pattern or a logo, people scroll past before they know what you sell.

Is the price or deal seen early?

For sale and discount ads, the offer should sit in spot 1 or 2. Buried deals don't stop the scroll.

Is a face helping or stealing?

Faces win the first look. That's good if the person looks at, holds or uses the product. It's bad if they lead eyes away from it.

Is it safe at the edges?

On Instagram and Facebook, captions, buttons and profile names cover the top and bottom. Keep key words away from the edges.

Be honest about what it is

A heatmap shows attention. Not sales.

What it's good at

  • Showing what stands out first and what gets lost
  • Telling you whether your product and offer actually get seen
  • Catching key words hidden under Reels and Stories buttons
  • Comparing versions with one focus score out of 100

What it can't tell you

  • Whether people will click, buy or fill a form
  • Whether your offer or message makes sense to them
  • Whether the ad breaks Meta, Google or Indian ad rules
  • Whether your landing page or targeting will waste money

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. No sign-up, no limit on how many ads you check.

Is my ad uploaded anywhere?

No. The heatmap is worked out inside your browser. Your image or video never leaves your phone or computer, and AdSure never sees it. Close the tab and it's gone.

How does it predict where people look?

It uses a model trained on real eye-tracking studies, where cameras recorded exactly where people looked at over a thousand images. The model learned what pulls the eye: faces, text, contrast and position. On slow connections we use a lighter, instant model instead, and the result says which one was used. It's a prediction, not a new eye-tracking study of your ad.

What does "Is your product seen?" do?

Draw a box around your product, offer, logo or button. We show what share of attention each one gets and the order people see them in, so you know whether the thing you're paying to show actually gets noticed.

Does it work for video ads?

Yes. It checks the opening frame, the one people see while they scroll, and one frame for each of the first 3 seconds, where most people decide to stay or scroll on.

Which ad sizes work?

Any. Square (1:1), portrait (4:5), stories and reels (9:16) and landscape all work.
